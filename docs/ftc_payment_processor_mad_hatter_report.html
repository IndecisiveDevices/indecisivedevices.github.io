Recommended default

Stripe first.
Cheddar Up if parent workflow matters more.

For your described volume, Stripe Payment Links is the cleanest baseline: no monthly fee, low card-link pricing, easy website buttons, QR codes, and professional sponsor checkout. Cheddar Up becomes the better operational tool when the coach wants registration fields, cash/check tracking, signups, waivers, and parent-facing collection pages.

1
Use clear buckets

Create separate collections or links for “Team Dues,” “Sponsor Support,” and “Robot Parts Fundraiser.” This keeps bookkeeping from becoming a confetti cannon.

2
Do not call it tax-deductible

Safer wording: “Support our FTC robotics team” or “Sponsor the Mad Botters.” Larger companies may still require a nonprofit or school channel.

3
Decide whether parents pay processing fees

Absorbing fees is cleaner. Passing fees protects budget. Either way, write the policy before payment links go live.

4
Keep two adult admins

Do not run payments through one person’s personal login or bank account. Unique logins and shared visibility matter more than a fancy checkout button.

Risk cabinet

Bank and account ownership

Use the real team bank account where possible. Keep at least two adult administrators with unique logins. Shared master logins are not a system; they are a haunted shoebox.

Refunds and chargebacks

Publish a dues/refund policy before collecting money. For example, spell out whether dues are refundable after registration fees, parts, shirts, or FTC expenses have been paid.

Sponsor language

Use “support,” “sponsorship,” or “contribution.” Do not imply tax deductibility without a qualified entity. You can still offer recognition benefits such as logos on the website, shirts, robot pit signage, or sponsor slides.

Fee pass-through

Passing fees to payers protects your budget, but some families dislike surprise add-ons. If used, state it plainly before checkout.
